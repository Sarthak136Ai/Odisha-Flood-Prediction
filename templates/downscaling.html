Statistical Spatial Downscaling Framework

District to Block Spatial Disaggregation

To bridge the spatial granularity gap between coarse regional precipitation signals (district spatial aggregates averaging ~5,190 km² across Odisha) and local rain gauge stations (averaging ~440 km² per block), a dedicated gradient-boosted statistical downscaling model (HistGradientBoostingRegressor) was trained and evaluated on 24 years of telemetry. The target resolution genuinely represents district-to-block point gauge disaggregation rather than unverified high-resolution gridded NWP simulation.

Key Scientific Findings

  • 1. Mathematical Areal Mean Nature: The coarse district baseline is the empirical centroid of station telemetry ($\text{Bias} \approx 0.00\text{ mm}$). Tree downscaling matches this unbiased behavior ($\text{Bias} = +0.0103\text{ mm}$ on test set) while preserving non-negative bounds ($\ge 0\text{ mm}$).
  • 2. Downstream Precision & PR-AUC Gain: Feeding downscaled inputs into the frozen flood prediction model increases Test PR-AUC from 0.1593 to 0.1813 (+0.0220) and improves precision from 19.05% to 22.96% (+3.91%), as spatial regularization dampens single-gauge sensor noise.
  • 3. Peak Intensity Underestimation: Convective cloudbursts (>115 mm/day) remain partially smoothed due to conditional mean regression shrinkage, a standard physical limitation of spatial disaggregation without radar or topographical grids.
